# FM-025 — a cold start reads thirty thousand tokens before it can work — the seat needs a brief

Seat: Principal · filed 2026-09-23 on the Owner's word after a cold Principal session on a metered harness hit its
usage limit with its work unpushed: *"That we must investigate and optimise for — the less token a cold-session startup
takes, the more valuable work can be achieved."* Written to be attacked.

## What is true now

**Filed 2026-09-23; nothing is built.** Measured in one consumer on 2026-09-23 (bytes ÷ 4): the pages a seat is told
to read before any durable act — the contract, the SOUL, the CREDO, the onboarding router, its own charter, the worktree
and branch doctrines — come to ≈ 21,000 tokens; the tracker reference alone ≈ 6,000 of them. `--next` prints ≈ 1,300;
`--owner` ≈ 100. The first tracker `--next` names is ≈ 9,000 tokens, the second ≈ 4,000. **A cold start therefore
spends 25,000–35,000 tokens reading before its first decision**, and every sub-agent it convenes (a Reviewer, an
Implementer) reads most of it again. On a harness with a session cap, that is the budget's first tenth gone to
orientation; the session in question spent 304,000 of a 500,000 cap and stopped before its push.

Nothing in the tool prints *what this seat must know now*: the current path, the seat's six lines (virtue, shadow,
takes, signs, may not), the session setup the gate demands, the gate commands, the top of `--next` with each item's
*Now* — in one read. `--next` is the closest; it is a queue, not a brief.

## Why

The Owner's measure for a session is what it achieves in its budget. A seat that reads for 30,000 tokens before
its first act, and whose every hand repeats the read, spends its budget on orientation the tool could give in two
thousand — and stops mid-chain when the cap comes, leaving unpushed work (the case above).

## Candidates — to be attacked, none chosen

1. **`--brief <seat>`:** one printout ≤ 2,000 tokens — the current path, the seat's six lines from the CREDO table
   (the consumer points the tool at its CREDO file, or the tool carries the generic six), the session setup lines the
   gate demands (`seat.session`, `--session open`), the full-gate commands, the first three of `--next` with their
   *Now*, `--owner`'s line, and the one rule per seat that burned last (from a consumer-kept list). The seat reads the
   long pages only where the brief points. Cost ≈ 60 lines and a configuration key naming the CREDO and the gate line.
2. **Shorter pages, no tool change:** the consumer trims its charters and the router to a page each; the tracker
   reference becomes lookup, never a pre-read. Cost: the consumer's, ongoing.
3. **The prompt carries the brief** (what the Owner did tonight by hand): no tool change; every Owner writes it again.
4. **Drop it.**

## What would decide it

- Tokens from session start to first durable act, per seat, before and after — ten cold starts each.
- Whether a seat that read only the brief breaks a rule the long pages hold (the misses ledger says which rules burn).
- Whether the sub-agents' repeated reads fall (they can be given the brief in their spawn prompt).

## Done when

The Owner has ruled the candidate; a cold seat's first durable act comes after ≤ 5,000 tokens of reading in the
measured consumer, with no rise in the misses the long pages exist to prevent, over ten starts.
