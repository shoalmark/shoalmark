### The shipwright and the inspector: yard roles, crew roles, or both

- **The shipwright is both** (input · W+J).
  - By its plain meaning it is a yard trade: "a person who designs, builds and repairs ships, especially wooden ones";
    shipbuilding "normally takes place in a specialized facility known as a shipyard".
  - It is also a crew role: the ship's carpenter was "a post aboard ships, both naval and commercial", and "the
    majority qualified as shipwrights in the dockyards before going to sea".
  - So in the yard of Addendum 1, where the vessel's own crew works on it, the shipwright reads as the crew's own
    shipwright as readily as the yard's.
- **The inspector is neither yard-bound nor crew** (input · W+J). It is an outside role in both settings:
  - in the yard, a ship in a dry dock "can be freely inspected or serviced";
  - in operation, port state control is "an inspection regime" that inspects ships "in port".
  - The plain word names no setting: "a person employed to inspect something".
  - It is not crew in either setting, which fits a reviewer that may not rewrite what it reviews. Its German form on
    the landing, *der Gutachter*, is recorded as the marine surveyor (FM-006, line 98), a yard and port figure.

## Q2: the coordinator's epithet and an icon brief

The coordinator sits between the planners, never above them: it commands none of them, a go from it grants nothing,
and authority stays in the Owner's signed answers. hail/1 is its protocol; the radio is only the transport channel.

### What traffic services do, and where informing ends (W)

- **The range:** a vessel traffic service is "a service implemented by a competent authority designed to improve the
  safety and efficiency of vessel traffic". In the maritime organisation's words, such services "range from the
  provision of simple information messages to ships to extensive management of traffic within a port or waterway".
  Ships may be "given advice on when to proceed", and "decisions concerning effective navigation and manoeuvring of the
  vessel remained with the ship's master" (its account of the 1985 guidelines; the current guidelines are resolution
  A.1158(32), not read here).
- **The line, in the standard phrases' message markers:**
  - INFORMATION "is restricted to observed facts", and its consequences "will be up to the recipient";
  - a WARNING informs "about danger", and its consequences "will be up to the recipient";
  - ADVICE influences "by a Recommendation", and "the decision whether to follow the ADVICE still stays with the
    recipient";
  - INSTRUCTION influences "by a Regulation": the sender "must have full authority to send such a message", and "the
    recipient has to follow this legally binding message unless he/she has contradictory safety reasons".
- **The line falls at INSTRUCTION:** a binding message from a sender with full authority. INFORMATION, WARNING and ADVICE
  sit on the informing side, where the recipient decides.
- **The coordinator sits on the informing side,** and its epithet must too. That rules out a controller: an air traffic
  controller's approach position "issues instructions to aircraft", and a traffic service's controller may send an
  INSTRUCTION. It leaves the operator who sends and receives.
- **A go from the coordinator** is the traffic service's "advice on when to proceed", never a clearance (J). The row's
  words should say *informs and advises* and avoid *control*.

### Does the signaller blur with the herald?

This seat is the herald, so this answer grades its own epithet. Yes, the two blur, in three ways:

- **The job:** both carry word and decide nothing. The name round already said the two "could blur".
- **The words:** "one who signals" and "a messenger, especially one bringing important news" are both bare carriers of
  a message.
- **The picture:** each would be drawn with an instrument held out from the head, the herald's horn or the signaller's
  flags.

They differ only in direction: the herald faces strangers outside the house, the signaller faces the ships inside the
fleet. Neither word carries that difference. The radio operator blurs less: its headset is worn, two-way and
symmetric, and its sense is to send *and receive*; the herald's horn is held, one-way and out to one side.

## Q3: the Owner's act on an ask

### The recount (M)

- **Method:** `scratch/recount.py` in this workbench.
  - A record is a block of consecutive non-blank lines inside a tracker's `## Asks` section, from that heading to the
    next level-2 heading, outside code fences.
  - An answer is a block with a line starting `**answered** — `. Its relation is a line starting `**relation** — ` in
    the same block.
  - The two lines in FM-050 (lines 95 and 107) that carry the marker inside prose are not records.
- **Result:** 158 tracker files; 29 with an `## Asks` section; 27 holding answers; **49 answers**, of which **47 carry a
  relation:**
  - 34 accepted the proposal;
  - 9 accepted with a change;
  - 3 chose another option;
  - 1 rejected.
- **The 2 answers that carry none:** FM-031's record of 2026-09-24 (line 258) and FM-032's of the same day (line 239).
  Neither has a `**signed** —` line either.

  - 5 records read `**withdrawn** — no answer was given` (FM-005, FM-059 twice, FM-100, FM-153): asks cleared without

  - 7 answers stand in front matter and are not yet cleared into a record (FM-004, FM-083, FM-084, FM-143, FM-148,
