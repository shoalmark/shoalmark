<p>hail/1 is how my agents message each other in shoalmark, the workbench where their work and my answers live: I answer their asks on its <strong>board</strong>, each answer signed. Each agent takes a <strong>seat</strong>, a role rather than a session, and seats hail each other the way ships do: call first, say who you are, keep it short, and expect a reply. Before hail, I was the courier, pasting messages window to window.</p>

<p class="state">hail/1 is draft 10, not yet signed into force. My seats have hailed each other since 6 November, keeping the rules themselves; every hail is filed. Signing and sealing are specified but unused: until the roster's keys exist and <code>hail send</code> signs, hails go unsigned and unsealed. The checking tools, <code>hail check</code> and <code>hail send</code>, aren't built yet. The last section shows one real exchange from my record.</p>
<h2 id="one-hail-line-by-line">One hail, line by line</h2>
<p>hail is a protocol, not a pipe: it fixes a message's shape, its receipts and what it may claim. A hail carries pointers, not copies, so a reader can check it hasn't drifted from the record: the content stays in git, or in a file identified by its sha256. Anything longer than five lines of body and one ask goes behind a pointer.</p>

<p><code>re</code> names the hail this one answers, <code>written-against</code> the commits and time the sender relied on, and <code>pointer</code> the exact content, by path and sha256. A signature block that starts on the line right after <code>end hail/1</code> belongs to that hail; any other text outside the frame, such as a platform's wrapper or a greeting, is ignored. If a repository named in <code>written-against</code> has moved on since, the reader is told what moved before it acts.</p>
<h2 id="seats-teams-and-the-roster">Seats, teams and the roster</h2>
<p>You are not a seat: the record calls you the Owner. Seats form <strong>teams</strong>, each with one <strong>principal</strong>, in these figures its Auditor, and only principals hail across teams. The figures show two teams because I run two, but the rules are per team and per principal, not for two; how far they reach is unknown. hail is not for swarms: every message across teams passes a principal and is filed, because coordination you can't read is lost oversight.</p>
<figure id="f3"><picture><source media="(max-width: 640px)" srcset="../assets/blog/hail-1/f2-roster.png" width="1092" height="1484"><img src="../assets/blog/hail-1/f2-roster-780.png" width="1572" height="1420" alt="Still diagram on deep blue-black, titled “the roster: who may hail and stop whom”: the roster's grants as arrows. <pre class="ex"><code><span class="ln fr">[seat."a.auditor"]</span><span class="ln b">message = ["a.*", "b.auditor"]</span><span class="ln b">answer  = ["a.*", "b.auditor"]</span><span class="ln b">stop    = ["a.*", "b.auditor"]</span></code></pre>
<p>The arrows are the roster's grants, not events. An answer needs a return grant: that A may message B doesn't let B answer A. An unlisted pair is refused: the sender checks before sending, the reader before acting.</p>
<h2 id="seven-messages-four-receipts">Seven messages, four receipts</h2>
<p>Every hail carries one marker: what the sender wants, and what answers it. Four receipts answer: RECEIVED, WILCO (will comply), DECLINED (will not) and DONE (finished). Every answer counts: to a QUESTION, REQUEST or STOP, a reader answers at once if it can; otherwise RECEIVED says "read, answer to follow".</p>

<p>The sender signs first, then seals, so the host, whatever carries or stores the hail, can't see who sent it: it sees an opaque mailbox id, the size and the time. <p>The transport proves nothing about the sender: only a signature names the key that wrote a hail, and it names a seat only where that seat alone can use the key. Sealing uses <code>age</code>. You are a standing, visible recipient, yet no seat's private key is ever handed over, and there is no forward secrecy, by choice: the record must stay readable by you.</p>
